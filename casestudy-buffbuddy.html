01 · Overview

The gym is a psychological barrier

For many people, going to the gym isn't just a physical challenge — it's a psychological one. Gym intimidation, as described by Nitschke (2023), is the insecurity, anxiety, and fear of judgement that makes exercising in public spaces overwhelming.

This challenge inspired BuffBuddy — a fitness app designed to make working out feel less intimidating and more supportive. Structured workout plans, mood-based motivation, and a friendly virtual "hype buddy" guide beginners step by step. By addressing emotional barriers like low self-esteem, lack of structure, and fear of burnout, BuffBuddy helps users build confidence, stay consistent, and develop a healthier relationship with fitness.

Findings

Critical
72.7% misclick rate on the live motivation toggle. Users found it only after multiple tries — the toggle was ambiguous and placed unexpectedly. Most users only found it on the 6th click or later.
Major
The dedicated "Mindfulness" page felt redundant. Users wanted a fitness tracking page instead. One participant directly stated: "The 'Mindfull' tab is out of place, I thought this was a fitness app."
07 · Iteration

Back to the drawing board — four changes

The principle behind the iterations

Every change was driven directly by what testing revealed, not by assumption. Each iteration maps to a specific failure mode identified in Phase 2.

Change 1 — Live Motivation Toggle

The toggle was repositioned and given clear context labelling. The 72.7% misclick rate dropped significantly after making the toggle more prominent and adding descriptive text around it.

Before: ambiguous toggle with heatmap showing 72.7% misclick rate scattered across screen
Before — 72.7% misclick
After: repositioned toggle with clear "Live Motivation" label and context description
After — discoverable

Change 2 — Mindfulness → Confidence Tracking

The standalone "Mindfulness" tab was removed entirely. Confidence and mood tracking was nested directly into the fitness stats page — making it feel like a natural extension of fitness progress rather than a separate product.

Change 3 — Confidence Check-in Question

The main check-in question and available mood options were updated based on user feedback. Font sizes were increased across the app to improve readability — a direct response to accessibility concerns raised during guerilla testing.

Before/After: check-in question updated, emoji options refined, font size increased throughout

Change 4 — Workout Goals & Rewards Separation

Sample goals were created to show how the hype buddy motivates in and out of the gym. "Fitness" and "Stats" sections were separated to improve clarity and ease of access — users were conflating the two.

08 · Design System

A system built for consistency

To ensure every screen felt cohesive and intentional, a simple design system was built before high-fidelity production. Every component maps back to the emotional design principles — energetic but approachable, structured but not clinical.

#006E6D — Teal
Balance and growth. Used for primary actions, progress indicators, and positive feedback states.
#EF476F — Coral
Energy and motivation. Used for the hype buddy, alerts, and high-energy moments in the workout flow.
#5579C6 — Link Blue
Used for interactive link text and navigation elements throughout the app.
#006E37 — Success Green
Positive confirmations, completed states, and goal achievements.
Inter · 16px base
Chosen for readability. 16px base ensures legibility under physical exertion — when screens are glanced at quickly.

Components — buttons, cards, and interactive toggles — were standardised for reuse across screens, ensuring the interface felt consistent regardless of which flow a user was in.

09 · Results

The design changes worked

"The app's usability score increased from 60% to 85% — and participants reported feeling more confident, less intimidated, and better supported."

The iterative testing approach paid off directly. Two phases of testing and four targeted redesigns produced measurable improvements in both usability metrics and how participants described their experience.

60% → 85% Usability Score
Two rounds of testing and four targeted redesigns produced a 25-point improvement in overall task completion rate.
4 out of 5 users
Said they would integrate BuffBuddy into their daily gym routine — driven by the buddy system and non-intimidating onboarding.
Fewer errors, more confidence
Participants completed all five tasks with significantly fewer errors. Qualitative feedback shifted from confusion to confidence.
"Confident" and "supported"
The most common words participants used to describe how the app made them feel — a direct result of the tone and Hype Buddy feature.
10 · Next Steps

Where BuffBuddy goes next

The current version proves the concept. The roadmap focuses on three directions that build on the emotional design foundation — expanding reach, deepening personalisation, and pushing the format into new contexts.

⌚
Watch Integration
Live motivation from your hype buddy on your wrist. Custom notifications, haptics, and progress rings synced to your goals — keeping you motivated in and out of the gym.
👥
Hype Squad
Unlock new hype buddies to create a whole squad. Each buddy has individual strengths that better match user goals, with confidence-based workout tailoring and a social sharing layer.
🥽
AR/VR — Far Future
An AR bubble at the gym to help users focus and cut out distractions — while your hype buddy checks your form, keeps count, and cheers you on during every rep.

11 · Reflection

What this project taught me

BuffBuddy reinforced that the most important thing in UX is listening — not just to what users say, but to what their behaviour reveals. The 72.7% misclick rate wasn't visible in interviews. It only appeared when we watched people actually use the product.

Working in a team of four also sharpened my ability to advocate for design decisions with evidence rather than opinion. When we proposed removing the Mindfulness tab, it was a significant structural change — but the user data made it an easy argument to make.

Most importantly, this project demonstrated that emotional design isn't a "nice to have" layer applied over a functional product. When the emotional barriers are the problem, emotional design is the solution. BuffBuddy didn't just help users work out — it helped them feel safe enough to try.
