BuffBuddy — Tackling gym intimidation through design

A fitness app that makes working out feel less intimidating and more supportive. Designed with mood-based motivation, structured plans, and a virtual hype buddy to help beginners build confidence and stay consistent.

01 · Overview

The gym is a psychological barrier

For many people, going to the gym isn't just a physical challenge — it's a psychological one. Gym intimidation, as described by Nitschke (2023), is the insecurity, anxiety, and fear of judgement that makes exercising in public spaces overwhelming.

This challenge inspired BuffBuddy — a fitness app designed to make working out feel less intimidating and more supportive. Structured workout plans, mood-based motivation, and a friendly virtual "hype buddy" guide beginners step by step. By addressing emotional barriers like low self-esteem, lack of structure, and fear of burnout, BuffBuddy helps users build confidence, stay consistent, and develop a healthier relationship with fitness.

02 · Users

Two students, one shared fear

Two personas anchored the project. Both are university students who want to exercise but are held back by how the gym makes them feel — not by a lack of motivation to be healthy.

Jenna wants company and a more enjoyable experience; Jake needs structure and reassurance. Together they pointed to the same design question: how do you make the first step feel safe?

03 · Focus

Three problems, three features

Rather than build everything, the team narrowed to three areas where the research was clearest — lack of structure, low self-confidence, and low motivation — and mapped a feature to each.

Auto-generated workouts
Workouts are based on the user's confidence level and the needs they input, so there is always a clear place to start.
Hype buddy with opt-in live encouragement
A virtual companion that keeps the user focused and gives live support during a workout — only when they want it.
Achievable goals that earn cosmetics
Completing fitness goals unlocks new looks for the buddy, adding motivation without judgement. AI machine scanning was scoped as an in-app bonus.
04 · The Hype Buddy

Encouraging, never judgmental

The buddy is the heart of the product, so its personality was defined before it was designed. It had to feel approachable rather than like another source of pressure.

Two decisions mattered most. Streaks were deliberately avoided, because they can leave users feeling discouraged (Dural and Kohls, 2017). And live motivation is opt-in, so users decide how much of the buddy they want.

05 · Ideation

From paper sketches to information architecture

Early exploration happened on paper. The first sketches split the home screen between fitness and mindfulness and tried different ways to ask "how are you feeling?" — a mood row near the top kept appearing in every version.

With a direction chosen, the information architecture mapped how onboarding, fitness, mindfulness, exploration and stats connect — including the filtering logic behind workout selection.

The first lo-fi screens combined fitness, mindfulness and upcoming sessions in a single landing state, switching between states with a swipe.

06 · Testing Phase 1

Guerilla testing reshaped the product

The first round put lo-fi screens and individual elements in front of 25 people from varied backgrounds, focusing on layout, feature priorities, accessibility concerns, and where existing features could grow.

The feedback changed the direction in three ways:

Soundscapes out, cheer buddy in
The 50/50 split between fitness and a mindfulness soundscape did not appeal to participants, so the soundscape approach was swapped for the cheer-buddy concept.
Larger buttons with visual cues and labels
Small labelled buttons were replaced with larger ones combining a visual representation and a label. Nonspecific icons became SF Symbols with labels so sections were clearer.
A customisable Quick Start
Users could now tailor their own quick-start shortcuts instead of using a fixed set.
07 · Low to Hi-Fi

Giving the buddy a face

The low-fidelity wireframes set structure: a check-in, a workout list, and a live-motivation screen with an exercise video, timer and a buddy. High-fidelity work then added the illustrated hype buddy, the teal system, and real workout photography.

08 · Testing Phase 2

What a usability study revealed

The high-fidelity prototype went through a second round with 16 participants, measured with the Post-Study System Usability Questionnaire (PSSUQ) alongside task observation.

Each participant completed five core tasks:

Create a workout
Select exercises, set reps and sets, name and save the workout. Primary navigation task.
Customise the hype buddy outfit
Find buddy settings and apply a new outfit. Tests discoverability of personalisation.
Complete the quick start quiz
Answer the onboarding confidence questions. Tests clarity of the quiz flow.
Enable live motivation
Switch on live hype buddy coaching during a workout.
View fitness statistics
Find and interpret the progress dashboard. Tests navigation and data legibility.

Findings

72.7%
Misclick rate on the live motivation toggle — nearly three in four taps landed elsewhere
6th
Click or later — when most people found the toggle

The live motivation toggle was ambiguous. It was a small switch in the top corner with a short label, and participants specifically said it was unclear. Most found it only on the sixth click or later.

The "Mindfulness" tab felt out of place. Participants wanted fitness tracking there instead. One put it bluntly: "The 'Mindfull' tab is out of place — I thought this was a fitness app."

09 · Iteration

Back to the drawing board — four changes

The principle behind the iterations

Every change was driven by what testing revealed, not by assumption. Each one maps to a specific problem found in the usability study.

Change 1 — Live motivation toggle

The toggle moved out of the header and into its own labelled card beneath the timer, with a plain-language description: "Get real-time motivation from your hype buddy during workouts." The primary action was also relabelled from "Start Exercise" to "Begin Workout".

Change 2 — Mindfulness → Confidence tracking

The standalone "Mindfulness" tab was removed. Confidence tracking now lives inside Stats as a seven-day view, so it reads as part of fitness progress instead of a separate product — with a summary line such as "Your confidence has improved 60% this week!"

Change 3 — Check-in question and readability

"Check in with your mood", with options like Low Energy and Anxious, became "How confident are you feeling?" with Uncertain, Nervous, Neutral, Optimistic and Confident — a question that matches the app's purpose. The buddy greeting became "hype buddy" and font sizes were increased across the app, a direct response to the accessibility feedback from guerilla testing.

Change 4 — Goals, rewards and clearer separation

Sample goals were added to show how the hype buddy could motivate users in and out of the gym, with rewards that unlock new buddy looks. "Fitness" and "Stats" were separated because users were treating them as one thing.

10 · Final Design

The final screens

The finished flow runs from a mood check-in and buddy customisation, through fitness and stats, to a structured workout plan — with live motivation available whenever the user wants it.

11 · Design System

A system built for consistency

To ensure every screen felt cohesive and intentional, a simple design system was built before high-fidelity production. Every component maps back to the emotional design principles — energetic but approachable, structured but not clinical.

#006E6D — Teal
Balance and growth. Used for primary actions, progress indicators, and positive feedback states.
#EF476F — Coral
Energy and motivation. Used for the hype buddy, alerts, and high-energy moments in the workout flow.
#5579C6 — Link Blue
Used for interactive link text and navigation elements throughout the app.
#006E37 — Success Green
Positive confirmations, completed states, and goal achievements.
Inter · 16px base
Chosen for readability. 16px base ensures legibility under physical exertion — when screens are glanced at quickly.

Components — buttons, cards, and interactive toggles — were standardised for reuse across screens, ensuring the interface felt consistent regardless of which flow a user was in.

12 · Results

The design changes worked

"The app's usability score increased from 60% to 85% — and participants reported feeling more confident, less intimidated, and better supported."

The iterative testing approach paid off directly. Two phases of testing and four targeted redesigns produced measurable improvements in both usability metrics and how participants described their experience.

60% → 85% Usability Score
Two rounds of testing and four targeted redesigns produced a 25-point improvement in the overall usability score.
4 out of 5 users
Said they would integrate BuffBuddy into their daily gym routine — driven by the buddy system and non-intimidating onboarding.
Fewer errors, more confidence
Participants completed all five tasks with significantly fewer errors. Qualitative feedback shifted from confusion to confidence.
"Confident" and "supported"
The most common words participants used to describe how the app made them feel — a direct result of the tone and Hype Buddy feature.
13 · Next Steps

Where BuffBuddy goes next

The current version proves the concept. The roadmap focuses on three directions that build on the emotional design foundation — expanding reach, deepening personalisation, and pushing the format into new contexts.

⌚
Watch Integration
Live motivation from your hype buddy on your wrist. Custom notifications, haptics, and progress rings synced to your goals — keeping you motivated in and out of the gym.
👥
Hype Squad
Unlock new hype buddies to create a whole squad. Each buddy has individual strengths that better match user goals, with confidence-based workout tailoring and a social sharing layer.
🥽
AR/VR — Far Future
An AR bubble at the gym to help users focus and cut out distractions — while your hype buddy checks your form, keeps count, and cheers you on during every rep.
14 · Reflection

What this project taught me

BuffBuddy reinforced that the most important thing in UX is listening — not just to what users say, but to what their behaviour reveals. The live motivation toggle problem barely came up in conversation; it only showed up when we watched people try to use it.

Working in a team of four also sharpened my ability to advocate for design decisions with evidence rather than opinion. When we proposed removing the Mindfulness tab, it was a significant structural change — but participants had told us directly that it felt out of place.

Most importantly, this project showed that emotional design isn't a "nice to have" layer applied over a functional product. When the emotional barriers are the problem, emotional design is the solution. BuffBuddy didn't just help users work out — it helped them feel safe enough to try.
