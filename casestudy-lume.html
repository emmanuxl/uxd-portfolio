LUME — A premium fashion store where the product is the design

A modern fashion e-commerce website that balances editorial storytelling with frictionless shopping. The central design challenge: build an experience that feels premium without competing with the clothing it sells.

01 · Overview

When restraint is the design

LUME is a modern fashion e-commerce website designed to showcase premium, sustainable clothing while providing a smooth and intuitive shopping experience. The project focused on creating a clean, editorial-style interface that balances brand storytelling with clear purchase paths across browsing, product discovery, and checkout.

Brand storytelling
LUME needed to communicate aspiration — seasonal collections, editorial photography, brand values — without burying the product.
Purchase clarity
Every screen had to maintain a clear path to purchase. No confusing navigation, no dead ends, no friction between browsing and buying.
Editorial restraint
The interface had to get out of the way of the clothing. Neutral palette, generous whitespace, and restrained typography throughout.

02 · Design Principles

Four rules that shaped every screen

Before touching any screen, four principles were established to guide every decision. These aren't goals — they're constraints. Constraints that kept the design honest.

01
The product wins
The interface should never compete with the clothing. Neutral palette, generous whitespace, and restrained typography exist solely to make the imagery stand out.
02
Discovery feels effortless
Users shouldn't have to work to find things. Filter placement, navigation structure, and sorting are all optimised to reduce scanning time and cognitive effort.
03
Trust before conversion
Every friction point in the purchase journey is mitigated by a trust signal nearby — shipping, returns, sustainability, secure checkout. Placed where doubt appears, not in the footer.
04
Familiar patterns, elevated
Users already know how e-commerce works. LUME uses those conventions — left filters, sticky nav, slide-in cart — but executes them with the attention to detail a premium brand demands.
03 · Screens & Design Decisions

Five pages, one clear intention each

Rather than designing screens and then explaining them, each page was built around a single primary job to be done — and every design decision flows from that job.

Communicate who LUME is in under 5 seconds, then get out of the way.

Key decisions
Large hero section immediately communicates brand tone and seasonal context — no ambiguity about what LUME sells or who it's for

Trust signals placed high (free shipping, easy returns, secure payments, sustainability) — reducing pre-purchase anxiety before users have even browsed
Collections grid and featured products support exploration without overwhelming — users who want to browse can, users who know what they want can navigate directly
Social proof layer (20,000+ customers, reviews) and @lume Instagram grid build community and aspiration — seen before checkout, not after

Primary job

Remove every reason a user might hesitate before adding to cart.

Key decisions
Large product imagery as the dominant element — buying fashion online is inherently a visual act, so the image gets the most real estate on the page
Concise, scannable product information — name, price, description, rating. No walls of text. Enough to decide, not enough to overwhelm

Trust elements (free shipping, free returns, secure checkout) placed directly below the CTA — at the exact moment where hesitation is most likely

04 · Visual & UI System

A palette that disappears

The visual system for LUME
The visual system for LUME was built around deliberate neutrality. In fashion e-commerce, a strong brand colour competes with product photography. The solution was a system where the UI recedes and the imagery advances.
Strong typographic hierarchy does the
Strong typographic hierarchy does the heavy lifting. Headlines are set large and light — editorial rather than bold. Product names and prices are clearly differentiated in weight and size so users can scan a grid and immediately understand the hierarchy of information without reading each item individually.
Three accessibility principles guided every
Three accessibility principles guided every component decision: clear contrast between text and background at all times, large tappable CTAs that work equally well on desktop and mobile viewports, and predictable navigation and layout patterns so returning users never feel disoriented. LUME should work as well on a phone on the go as it does on a desktop at home.
Neutral White — Background
Off-white rather than pure white reduces eye strain and gives product photography a warmer, more editorial quality.
Near Black — Text & CTAs
Deep charcoal instead of pure black for body text and primary CTAs. Strong contrast without the harshness of #000000 on light backgrounds.

05 · Outcomes

What LUME demonstrates

"The best e-commerce design is invisible. Users should only notice the products — never the interface."

🎯
Conversion-aware design — every page maps to a specific user job, with trust signals and CTAs placed to address hesitation at its most likely moment
🖼️
Editorial restraint — a neutral, image-forward visual system that communicates premium quality without the UI competing with the product photography
🔄
Familiar patterns, elevated — established e-commerce conventions (filters, sticky nav, slide-in cart) executed with the attention to detail a premium brand demands

Reflection

LUME pushed back against one of the most common UX instincts: the desire to make the design noticeable. In most product work, you want your interface to communicate craft. In fashion e-commerce, you want it to communicate the brand — which means getting out of the way almost entirely.

The tension between brand storytelling (the homepage, the commitment section, the Instagram grid) and shopping efficiency (filters, quick-add, slide-in cart) was the most interesting design problem in the project. They pull in opposite directions — one wants users to linger and feel something, the other wants them to convert quickly. The solution was sequencing: earn the browse on the homepage, then serve the efficiency once they've decided to shop.

This project also reinforced how much conversion depends on removing friction rather than adding persuasion. The low-stock cues and trust signals aren't trying to convince anyone — they're removing the last reasons not to buy, at exactly the moment those reasons appear.
