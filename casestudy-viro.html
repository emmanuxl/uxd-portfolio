01 · Overview

Waste collection is broken for everyone involved

Viro is a mobile app that connects customers with verified waste collectors and drivers for on-demand waste collection. The platform supports three distinct user roles — Customer, Collector, and Driver — each with a tailored experience, all staying connected through real-time job status updates.

The objective was to design a scalable, mobile-first product that simplifies waste collection for customers while simultaneously supporting operational efficiency for collector companies and their drivers. This wasn't just one app — it was three, built as one.

Customer
Requests waste collection on-demand. Needs full transparency from booking to completion — scheduling, status updates, and payment all in one flow.
Collector
Manages incoming job requests and assigns drivers. Needs operational clarity — acceptance controls, fleet overview, and earnings reporting.
Driver
Executes on assigned pickups. Needs route efficiency and real-time job status — location guidance, checklists, and instant communication with the collector.

02 · Problem

Three broken experiences in one industry

Waste collection services are failing all three stakeholders simultaneously. The customer can't see what's happening, the collector can't manage efficiently, and the driver can't act quickly. Designing for one without the others just shifts the problem.

Rigid scheduling
Services are difficult to book on-demand. Customers must plan days in advance with no flexibility for urgent needs.
No visibility
Zero insight into pickup status. Once a request is made, customers are left waiting with no updates or ETA.
Operational chaos
Collectors manage requests through calls and spreadsheets. No system for driver assignment or real-time job tracking.

Design Goals

Rather than designing for the loudest stakeholder, the brief required a solution that worked for all three simultaneously — which meant finding a shared system logic that could serve radically different needs.

G1 · Customer
Enable waste collection requests quickly and confidently — from any device, in any location.
G2 · Collector
Help companies manage incoming demand, assign drivers efficiently, and monitor performance.
G3 · Driver
Give drivers the information they need instantly — without cognitive overload while on the road.
G4 · All roles
Keep every party aligned through consistent, real-time feedback throughout the collection lifecycle.

04 · Onboarding

Setting the right expectations from the start

The onboarding flow introduces Viro's core value and sets expectations early. Rather than presenting a generic sign-up, users select their role upfront — this single decision shapes every screen they see afterwards.

Three key design decisions kept onboarding clean: progressive disclosure (features revealed only when relevant), contextual permission requests (camera, location, and notifications asked when needed, not upfront), and early role selection to eliminate unnecessary complexity downstream.

06 · Real-Time Tracking

The same status, seen by everyone

To build trust and transparency, the app provides real-time status updates throughout the collection lifecycle. A consistent progress indicator is shared across all three roles — so the customer, collector, and driver always see the same ground truth.

Request Accepted
Collector has confirmed the job. ETA displayed to customer. Driver assigned.
En Route
Driver is travelling to the pickup location. Live map tracking active for customer.
Arrived
Driver has reached the location. Customer notified. Collection begins.
Collecting Waste
Active collection underway. Progress indicator updates in real time.
Collection Complete
Job done. Customer prompted to rate. Receipt generated. Collector earnings updated.

08 · Driver Experience

Built for speed, not screens

The driver flow prioritises clarity and speed above everything else. Drivers are on the move — they can't afford to navigate complex menus or interpret ambiguous UI while managing a vehicle. Every screen was stripped to its essential information.

The key design principle: one clear action per screen. Accept or decline. Navigate or wait. Start or complete. No decision fatigue, no hunting for buttons.

The Driver Flow

Dashboard
Total jobs, earnings, ratings, and today's step count at a glance.
Assignment notification
New job with waste details, customer location, and estimated pay — accept or decline.
Navigation
In-app route to pickup location with one-tap to open in Maps.
Collection checklist
Confirm waste type, photo the load, mark collection started.
Completion
Mark job done, trigger automatic payment, prompt collector rating.

09 · Design System

One visual language, three experiences

The design system was built
The design system was built around a single principle: reduce cognitive load. A clean, minimal visual style ensures that users across all three roles can act quickly without being overwhelmed by the interface.
Shared status indicators were the
Shared status indicators were the most critical design decision — by using the same visual language for collection states across Customer, Collector, and Driver views, every party interprets the same event identically. This eliminates confusion between what the customer sees and what the driver is experiencing.
Green — Trust & Action
Green communicates trust, sustainability, and active states. Used for CTAs, status indicators, and confirmed states across all three roles.
Dark — Clarity
High-contrast dark backgrounds keep information scannable — essential for drivers and collectors who need to act quickly in varied lighting conditions.
Components — Shared Logic
Consistent status indicators, request cards, and navigation patterns are shared across all three role views — so learning one role transfers to another.

10 · Outcomes

What Viro delivered

"Designing for three users simultaneously taught me that the real design work isn't in the individual flows — it's in the connective tissue between them."

📱
End-to-end product — a complete mobile marketplace covering onboarding, request, matching, tracking, and review for all three roles
🔄
Shared system design — consistent status indicators and feedback loops mean all parties always understand the same job state
⚡
Speed-first driver UX — the driver experience was stripped to single-action screens, designed for use while on the move

Reflection

Viro was the most architecturally complex project I've worked on solo. Designing for three distinct mental models within a single product meant constantly context-switching — stepping into the shoes of a customer one moment, a fleet manager the next, then a driver with limited screen time.

The biggest lesson was about feedback loops. In a service platform, the quality of the experience depends not on how polished each individual screen is, but on how reliably information flows between roles. When the driver updates a status, the customer sees it instantly, and the collector's records update automatically. That invisible chain is the product.

This project also reinforced the value of designing progressively — building the shared system (status states, request cards, navigation patterns) before branching into role-specific flows meant that each role's interface inherited coherence rather than having to create it from scratch.
