About

Why I design the way I do

I design with a simple belief: if a product confuses, excludes, or frustrates people, it's not finished.

My approach to UX started with a curiosity about how people think — how they make decisions, where they hesitate, and what gets in their way. Over time, that curiosity turned into a more structured, research-led process, shaped by seeing what happens when design is based on assumptions instead of real user insight.

Accessibility became a core part of my work through this process. The more I designed and evaluated products, the more I noticed how often people are unintentionally excluded — not because designers don't care, but because accessibility is treated as an afterthought. That shift in perspective pushed me to design more deliberately, guided by WCAG standards and grounded in real user needs.

What drives me is simple: designing things that don't just look good, but genuinely work for the people they're meant for.

How I Work

A process built on evidence

Every project follows a clear methodology — adapted to the team's pace, but never skipping the fundamentals.

Discover

User interviews, competitive analysis, heuristic evaluation. I build empathy before I open Figma or write a line of code.

Define

Problem framing, job stories, success metrics, and accessibility requirements. Alignment before divergence saves weeks.

Design & Build

Wireframes → hi-fi → production code. I can hand off specs or write the component myself — whichever serves the team.

Validate

Usability testing, A/B experiments, accessibility audits (WCAG 2.2, ARIA, screen readers). Ship with confidence, not hope.
